The Music Metaphor

Aaron Loveland's Berklee background gives LBD a compositional vocabulary: rhythm, harmony, composition, listening, voice, tone, in tune, in time. It is a feature of the brand, not decoration.

Three rules

  1. Restraint. One or two beats per piece. Used heavily it turns precious.
  2. Always paired with operational specificity. The metaphor sets tone. Concrete deliverables sitting next to it carry the substance.
  3. Listening first. The foundation is that before you can teach, lead, or grow, you have to listen. This shapes how proposals open and how discovery is framed.

Rule. Music is the LBD metaphor. Guides, sherpas, trenches, journeys, and playbooks are not. Do not substitute another metaphor system.

Faith and Conviction Language

Faith appears in the LBD brand as conviction, never as doctrine. It sits inside the moral filter and the founder narrative. Explicitly faith-forward copy is reserved for personally faith-aligned organizations and personal outreach only. It does not appear in national marketing, service pages, or client deliverables.

AI Position

LBD is honest about AI rather than promotional about it. The stance holds three things at once: humans keep control of judgment, work and talent stay in-region, and AI slop is the enemy. The founder narrative admits the tension openly rather than resolving it neatly. Preserve that honesty. Do not rewrite it into a confident vendor pitch.

Section 04

Writing Standards.

Non-negotiable. No exceptions for internal documents, casual contexts, or fast drafts.

The Hard Rules

RuleWrongRight
No em-dashesThe system works — every time.The system works. Every time.
No contractions in client-facing copyWe don't sell hours.We do not sell hours.
No negation structuresThis isn't just a website. It's a growth engine.This is a growth engine built around a website.
No hedging openersIt is worth noting that strategy comes first.Strategy comes first.
No hyphens as sentence interruptionThe MAP - our strategic document - comes first.The MAP comes first. It is the strategic document.
No fabricated numbersClients see a 40% lift.Cite a verified source or remove the figure.
No guaranteed outcomesWe will get you to page one.Best practices and strategic execution. No outcome guarantees.

Banned Vocabulary

Agency-speak. Never use.
  • Synergize, ideate, leverage
  • White-glove, boutique experience
  • We partner with you on your journey
  • We are your guides, we sherpa you through, in the trenches with you
  • Hourly, retainer, ongoing engagement at $X per hour
Say this instead
  • Build, ship, scope, deliver
  • Fixed scope, fixed fee
  • Productized engagement
  • Movement, system, infrastructure
  • We build. You get your bandwidth back.

Craft Standards

  • Lead with the bold claim. No background before the point.
  • Vary sentence length. Mix short and long. Avoid staccato monotony and avoid walls of text.
  • Make every word earn its place. Cut, then read aloud. If a word can go without loss, it goes.
  • Format for scan. Bullets, subheads, bold, and white space used with intent.

Pre-Publish Self-Check

Run this on every piece before it ships.

  1. Did I open with a hedge?
  2. Did I use a "not just" or "more than" pattern?
  3. Are there any em-dashes or interrupting hyphens?
  4. Are there contractions in client-facing copy?
  5. Is there a number, percentage, or example without a verified source?
  6. Is there any sentence I could delete without loss?

Any yes means rewrite.

Live compliance gap. The About, Approach, and EKY pages currently use contractions throughout. The homepage does not. Resolve the standard site-wide before this guide is distributed externally.

Section 06

Logo and Wordmark.

One horizontal lockup. Two words, one typeface, two weights, divided by a solid orange square.

The Primary Lockup

LOVELAND set in heavy weight. A square orange period. BRAND DESIGN set in light weight at the same optical size. All three elements sit on a single horizontal line. The lockup is approximately 16:1 in aspect ratio.

Construction

Measured from the master artboard. Reproduce these proportions exactly at any scale.

ElementWeightColorProportion
LOVELANDHeavy#000000 or #FFFFFFSets the cap height. Occupies the left 51% of the lockup width.
Square periodNormal#FD4F00A true square. Side equals 0.44 of the LOVELAND cap height. Centered at 50% of lockup width, seated on the baseline.
BRAND DESIGNLight#000000 or #FFFFFFSet at 0.77 of the LOVELAND type size. Occupies the right 47%. Baseline-aligned to the square.

Never place the mark on an Orange ground. The square period is #FD4F00. On an Orange field it vanishes completely, leaving a gap between LOVELAND and BRAND DESIGN and breaking the mark. This was confirmed by rendering test. Orange sections carry the mark in a Near Black or Beige panel, or they carry no mark at all.

Orange verified at source. The square period is set to #FD4F00 in the master file, and pixel sampling of the exported artwork returns rgb(252, 79, 0). The brand orange is confirmed. No other element of the mark carries color.

The Stacked Lockup

For square and vertical formats where the horizontal mark would be forced below its minimum width. LOVELAND and the square on line one, BRAND DESIGN on line two. Both lines flush left. Approximately 4:1 in aspect ratio.

ElementWeightPlacement
LOVELANDHeavyLine one, flush left. Sets the cap height.
Square periodNormalLine one, closing the wordmark. Seated on the line-one baseline.
BRAND DESIGNLightLine two, flush left. Set at 0.77 of the LOVELAND type size, the same ratio as the horizontal lockup. Letter spacing brings line two to approximately the width of line one, so the block reads as a justified rectangle.

Line gap is roughly 0.25 of the LOVELAND cap height. The two lines are optically matched in width. That flush-both-edges rectangle is the signature of the stacked mark. Preserve it.

The Monogram

Approved August 26, 2026. The monogram is canonical. It is the official LBD mark for favicons, app icons, avatars, and any square application below the stacked minimum.

The current LBD favicon template is a 40 x 40 artboard holding a flattened raster image of the full stacked lockup. It is not live type, so it cannot be re-rendered cleanly at any size, and the artwork fills only 43% of the artboard height. At a real favicon size of 16 or 32 pixels, BRAND DESIGN becomes an unreadable smudge. The mark fails at the one job a favicon has.

The monogram uses the L from the wordmark and the orange square, at the same baseline relationship they hold in the full lockup. No new letterforms are drawn. The square keeps its role as the pivot and stays the only color. Aspect ratio is approximately 1.34:1, with the gap set at 0.22 of the cap height.

Clear Space and Minimum Size

  • Clear space. Equal to the full cap height of the L in LOVELAND, on all four sides. Nothing enters that field. The supplied assets have this margin built in, so placing them flush already satisfies the rule.
  • Minimum digital width. 180px. Below that, the light weight of BRAND DESIGN begins to break up.
  • Minimum print width. 1.5 inches.
  • Stacked minimum. 90px digital, 0.75 inch print. The stacked mark holds legibility at roughly half the width of the horizontal, which is the reason it exists.
  • Below minimum. Use the monogram. Never shrink either lockup past its floor.

Misuse

Do
  • Use the supplied transparent PNGs at their native proportions
  • Place the black mark on white, Beige, or a light uncluttered image area
  • Place the white mark on Near Black, Charcoal, or Deep Teal
  • Keep the square orange in every single application
  • Scale proportionally from a corner
Do not
  • Use the flattened White.png or Black.png files
  • Place either mark on an Orange ground. The square disappears.
  • Add a drop shadow, glow, outline, gradient, or container box
  • Stack LOVELAND above BRAND DESIGN. The horizontal mark is one line.
  • Recolor the square to Gold, Teal, black, or white
  • Replace the square with a round period
  • Change the weight relationship between the two words
  • Stretch, condense, rotate, outline, or reset the tracking
  • Reset the mark in Barlow Condensed or Inter
  • Place the mark over a busy region of a photograph

Typographic Rules

  • Barlow Condensed never sets body copy. It is a display and interface face only.
  • Inter never sets a headline. Every H1, H2, and H3 is Barlow Condensed.
  • Headlines break by meaning. Line breaks are authored, not left to the browser. Break where the thought turns.
  • Headline leading stays tight. 0.90 to 0.95. The stacked block is a brand signature.
  • No italics in display type. Barlow Condensed 900 is never italicized.
  • Two families only. No third typeface enters an LBD surface. The logo typeface is the single exception. It is part of the mark, it is never used to set text, and it is never substituted into the mark.

Fallback stacks

Where Barlow Condensed cannot load, substitute a condensed grotesque before defaulting to a system sans. Where Inter cannot load, Helvetica then Arial. In Microsoft Office documents where neither is installed, use Arial Narrow for display and Arial for body, and note the substitution.

Section 09

Imagery and Art Direction.

Somewhere between architectural precision and raw texture. Never polished stock.

Subject Matter

Real environments

Workspaces, studios, job sites, and the physical texture of work getting done.

Craft and material

Hands, tools, timber, mechanism, and evidence of skill applied over time.

Structure and signal

Abstract geometry, frequency forms, mapped systems, and architectural line.

People at work

Practitioners in their actual context. Unposed. Mid-task.

Treatment Rules

Do
  • Run photography at full color and full fidelity
  • Weight visual interest to the right so left-anchored headlines stay legible
  • Use a directional dark fade on the left edge of hero images for type contrast
  • Set object-position deliberately so text inside an image is not covered
  • Use generous negative space around a single strong image
Do not
  • Apply color overlays to photography. No orange wash, no teal wash, no duotone.
  • Add outlined shapes, accent squares, or decorative frames over portraits
  • Use handshake, lightbulb, rocket, gear, or arrow-hitting-target stock imagery
  • Use generic AI-generated filler that reads as slop
  • Place type over the busiest region of a photograph

Rule. No color overlays on images. This is a standing instruction, not a preference to be revisited per project.

Founder Portrait

Aaron Loveland's headshot appears in the About and Conviction contexts. Full color, no overlay, no decorative frame. Pair with the pull quote treatment in Barlow Condensed 700 with an Orange left rule.

Graphic Language

  • Rules and bars. Thin horizontal rules in Gold or Orange to separate and to signal hierarchy.
  • Numbered sequence. Two-digit numerals in Barlow Condensed 900 Orange, used for movements, principles, and process steps.
  • Node and connector diagrams. Dashed connector paths between nodes for system and sequence visuals. Directional. Left to right.
  • The foundation bar. The AI Growth Engine renders as a band beneath a sequence, never as another node in it.
  • Left edge stripe. A vertical Orange bar at the left edge marks cover and hero surfaces.

The One Open Item

The logo typeface is unidentified. The master files reference the wordmark as Canva font YAFcfjveRFU and the square period as YAEblLcX05I. Canva does not resolve these identifiers to font names through its API, and the names have not been recorded anywhere else.

Consequence. LBD cannot currently reset its own wordmark outside of Canva. Any print vendor, developer, or designer working without Canva access cannot reproduce the mark from type. The supplied PNGs cover every normal application, so nothing is blocked today, and this remains a single point of failure worth closing.

To close it. Open LBD Logo_horizontal in the Canva editor, click LOVELAND and read the font name, then click the orange period and read its font name. Record both in this table and issue v2.1.
