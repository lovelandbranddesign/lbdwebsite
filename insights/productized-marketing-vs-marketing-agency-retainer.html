Productized Marketing vs. a Marketing Agency Retainer

November 10, 2026
In This Post
A marketing agency retainer buys hours. Productized marketing buys defined deliverables at a fixed fee. Compare scope, accountability, and change.
← All Insights
Start My MAP

Short answer: a marketing agency retainer buys access to an agency’s time. Productized marketing buys defined deliverables at a fixed fee. A retainer sets the price by the resources committed. A productized engagement sets the price by the work delivered, and it writes that work down before anything starts. Both can run for years, and they differ in what you can verify along the way.

What a marketing agency retainer is

A marketing agency retainer is a recurring monthly fee for an agreed block of agency resources. The client reserves capacity, and the agency fills it with work: campaigns, content, reporting, and requests as they arise. It is the most common structure among the agencies SE Ranking surveyed. SE Ranking’s 2023 pricing survey of 135 marketing and SEO agencies found that 32.6% use a monthly retainer as their only model. Another 45.2% combine monthly retainers with hourly or project pricing, which puts the total offering retainers at 77.8%. The authors note that the sample is too small for statistical significance, and 57% of respondents had fewer than five staff, so read it as a snapshot of small agencies.

Darren Woolley of the marketing consultancy TrinityP3 has written a detailed critique of the model. His essay, first published in 2018, lists ten problems with agency retainers. Among them: retainers reward the hours delivered and give the agency no incentive to improve performance; timesheets that lack project detail leave the client unable to tell whether the hours delivered match the hours paid for; and poor briefing, cancelled projects, and slow approvals consume retainer resources with no accountability. That is one practitioner’s analysis of the structure’s incentives.

What productized marketing is

Shopify defines a productized service as “a predefined, packaged service that’s sold like a physical good with a fixed price.” Instead of billing by the hour, the provider charges a fee for a package, and the package is defined by what the client receives.

At Loveland Brand Design, each of the seven movements is a productized engagement with a fixed scope and a fixed fee, with no hourly billing. Our own FAQ puts the difference in three parts: fixed scope, fixed fee, and a moral filter. Our earlier posts cover the definition of productized marketing and the billing difference between fixed fees and hourly rates. This post compares the engagement structures.

One distinction matters here. A 2024 4A’s survey, reported with the ANA, found fixed fee the most used compensation model among those polled. A fixed fee fixes the invoice. Only a fixed scope fixes the work. A productized engagement fixes both.

Four differences that matter

1. What the fee measures

A retainer fee measures capacity. The client pays for the resources reserved, so the natural question each month is whether the agency used them. A productized fee measures the work. Before a single deliverable is built, the client knows what it is getting, what it costs, and when it is done. Our FAQ states the principle directly: we do not sell hours.

2. Where the scope is written

In a productized engagement, the proposal is the contract. Scope, fee, and deliverables sit in one document that both sides can read at any point. The result is no surprise invoices, no scope creep, and no “that is an add-on.”

Billing surprises are a documented reason clients leave agencies. In a 2019 Digiday survey of 73 client-side marketers, 77% named underperforming or low-quality campaigns as a key reason for ending agency work, 29% cited overbilling, and 26% cited hidden fees. The sample is small and the survey is several years old, so treat the figures as directional. Two of the three reasons concern billing, and the third concerns results.

3. How change is handled

Every marketing engagement meets changes: a new product, a shifted priority, a request nobody scoped. Under a retainer, those requests draw on the same pool of hours, and Woolley’s critique points out that inefficiencies on the client side, such as slow approvals, consume the pool without anyone being accountable for them. Under a fixed scope, the rule is written down: if it is not in the proposal, we discuss it before we touch it. The conversation happens before the work, and the client decides with the full picture.

4. How long it runs

Long agency relationships are common. The ANA and the 4A’s reported in April 2025 that average client-agency tenure is about seven years, up from 3.2 years in 2016. Clients without mandatory agency reviews, 60% of respondents, reported relationships averaging 8.1 years. The release gives no sample size, and it covers both client-side marketers and agencies.

A productized model leaves room for long relationships and defines each stage inside them. Every new client begins with the Marketing Action Plan, and what follows depends on what the plan reveals. Some clients move through all seven movements over 12 to 18 months. Each movement has a defined scope and a defined finish.

A fixed fee fixes the invoice. Only a fixed scope fixes the work.

When a retainer fits

A retainer fits work that is continuous and cannot be defined in advance: standing public relations support, reactive creative requests, or a team that needs a resource on call. If the work arrives unpredictably, reserved capacity is a reasonable way to buy it.

Productized marketing fits when the outcome can be named: a plan, a brand, a website, a content system, a monthly advertising program with defined deliverables. A small business buying an outcome of that kind benefits from knowing the scope and the cost before the first deliverable starts.

How we handle ongoing work

Some marketing work repeats every month, and we handle it without a retainer. Our FAQ answers the question directly: we do not sell time. Digital Advertising and Prospecting and Pipeline involve ongoing monthly work, and they are priced as fixed-scope monthly engagements with defined deliverables. The deliverables set the value. The AI-Integrated Growth Engine runs through every movement and is included in the fixed fee at no extra cost.

Six questions to ask any agency before you sign

  1. What exactly will you deliver, and by when?
  2. What does the fee cover, and what sits outside it?
  3. What happens when I ask for something outside the scope?
  4. How will I know the work produced results?
  5. Who does the work, and does that person stay on my account?
  6. How does the engagement end, and what do I keep?

Our proposals answer the first three before any work begins. Ask all six of any agency, including us.

Frequently asked questions

What is a marketing agency retainer?

A marketing agency retainer is a recurring monthly fee for an agreed block of agency time and resources. The client reserves capacity, and the agency fills it with campaigns, content, reporting, and requests as they arise.

How is productized marketing different from a retainer?

A retainer sets the price by the resources committed. Productized marketing sets the price by the work delivered, with a fixed scope and a fixed fee written into the proposal before work begins.
