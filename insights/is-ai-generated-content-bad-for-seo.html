Is AI-Generated Content Bad for SEO in 2026?

September 8, 2026
In This Post
Google does not penalize content for being AI-written, but the March 2026 Core Update tells a sharper story. A practitioner Q&A on what actually happened and what it means.
← All Insights
Start My MAP

Short answer: no, not directly. Google does not have a rule that penalizes content for being AI-written. But the real 2026 ranking data tells a more specific story than that simple answer suggests, and it is worth walking through, question by question, the way it actually came up in conversation this week.

Does Google actually penalize content for being written by AI?

No. Google's own Search Central documentation is direct about this: content is evaluated on quality signals like Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trust, not on who or what produced the words. There is no AI-detection penalty built into how Google ranks pages. That part of the answer has not changed since we covered it in our last piece.

Then why does this question keep coming up?

Because the March 2026 Core Update gave everyone a real, measurable answer to a question that used to be theoretical. According to reporting on that update's impact, AI content farms lost 60-80% of their organic traffic in the rollout, while sites publishing original data gained 22% visibility. Human-written content held the number one search position 80% of the time after the update, compared to just 9% for purely AI-generated pages. Affiliate sites, which lean hardest on AI-scaled content, saw 71% experience ranking drops.

Does that mean AI-assisted content can't rank at all?

No, and this is the part people get wrong. A separate independent study of 600,000 ranking pages found the correlation between AI content percentage and Google ranking was 0.011, statistically close to zero. AI-assisted content ranks constantly. What the March update actually punished was scale without editorial oversight, pages produced in volume with nothing specific added, which is a description of AI slop, not a description of AI assistance.

So what actually got penalized, if not AI itself?

Volume without a human behind it. Google's policy on scaled content abuse targets exactly this: content generated at scale primarily to fill search results, with no meaningful review or original value added. That is the same five-pattern failure we named in our first piece on AI slop, generic phrasing, unattributed claims, no named human, repeated structure, no verifiable detail, just applied at the scale of hundreds or thousands of pages instead of one homepage.

What does this mean practically, for a small business owner using AI to help with content?

It means the tool was never the problem. A business owner using AI to draft faster, then reviewing it, adding a real name, a real number, a real detail only their business can claim, is doing exactly what the post-update data rewards. A business publishing AI output unreviewed, at volume, hoping quantity substitutes for substance, is doing exactly what the update punished. The difference is not the software. It is whether a real person stood behind the words before they went live.

How does LBD use AI without landing on the wrong side of that line?

Every piece of AI-assisted work here gets checked against the same five patterns before it publishes, generic phrasing, unattributed claims, no named human, repeated structure, no verifiable detail. That is not a slogan. It is the actual editorial standard this piece was written to.

No. Google's own documentation states it evaluates quality signals like Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trust, regardless of whether a human or a model produced the content. There is no direct AI-authorship penalty.

What happened to AI content in the March 2026 Core Update?

AI content farms lost 60-80% of their organic traffic. Sites publishing original data gained 22% visibility. Human-written content held the number one search position 80% of the time, compared to 9% for purely AI-generated pages.

Is there a real correlation between AI content and search rankings?

What actually gets AI-assisted content penalized?
